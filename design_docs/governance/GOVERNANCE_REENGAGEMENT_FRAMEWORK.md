# Governance Re-Engagement Framework

## Purpose

This document defines how the approved portfolio should restore risk after a governance-led temporary de-risk event.

The goal is capital restoration discipline, not alpha discovery.

## Re-Engagement Principle

Re-engagement must be:

- explicit
- simple
- reviewable
- state-aware
- clearly separate from trading signals

Re-engagement must not depend on inventing a new market-timing layer.

## De-Risk And Re-Engagement Ladder

### Level 0: `Full Approved Posture`

- `Core` active
- `Sleeve #1` at full approved deployment posture
- `Sleeve #2` at full approved deployment posture
- `3.0x` hard cap remains in force

This is the normal state.

### Level 1: `Partial Sleeve Restraint`

- `Core` unchanged
- one or both sleeves temporarily reduced in deployment posture
- stacking still allowed, but under reduced posture

Use case:

- elevated governance caution
- early-stage review after path-burden escalation
- operational stabilization phase

### Level 2: `Incremental Sleeves Reduced`

- `Core` unchanged
- both incremental sleeves materially reduced
- portfolio effectively operates closer to core-plus-light-overlays

Use case:

- serious but temporary governance intervention
- drawdown burden or cap-pressure persistence judged unacceptable for current live posture

### Level 3: `Core-Dominant Temporary Posture`

- `Core` remains
- sleeves remain logically valid but are minimally deployed or temporarily withheld

Use case:

- severe governance caution
- operational instability
- post-incident stabilization

This is a deployment posture, not a new formal portfolio ranking.

## Re-Engagement Styles Considered

### Rejected: automatic signal-like restoration

Why rejected:

- too easy to drift into hidden strategy logic
- too close to reopening frozen timing research

### Rejected: indicator-trigger re-entry ladder

Why rejected:

- turns governance into a covert signal layer
- too similar in spirit to frozen `Risk-Off` re-entry logic

### Preferred: review-cleared staged restoration

Why preferred:

- explicit and auditable
- operationally simple
- avoids pretending governance is a new alpha source

## Proposed Re-Engagement Method

### Step 1: `Intervention recorded`

When governance de-risks:

- record trigger category
- record posture level entered
- record start date
- record metrics that justified intervention

### Step 2: `Clearance check`

Re-engagement can only begin when all of the following are true:

- alert state has improved from `Escalate` or `Review` to a lower posture
- no hard-cap or operational incident remains open
- path-burden metrics have stopped deteriorating
- governance explicitly clears restoration

### Step 3: `Staged restoration`

Preferred restoration sequence:

1. restore `Sleeve #2` first if the original intervention was mainly cap-pressure or path-burden related
2. restore `Sleeve #1` next after stable review clearance
3. return to full approved posture only after one full review cycle without renewed deterioration

Why this order:

- `Sleeve #2` is the diversification sleeve
- `Sleeve #1` is the main gain sleeve and usually carries more practical upside / path stress tradeoff

### Step 4: `Hold-and-confirm window`

After any restoration step:

- keep posture unchanged until the next scheduled governance review
- do not oscillate posture rapidly

This prevents governance churn from becoming pseudo-timing.

## Restoration Decision Rules

### Immediate full restoration

Allowed only when:

- intervention was driven by operational caution rather than path distress
- the caution source has been clearly resolved
- governance explicitly signs off

### Staged restoration

Default method when:

- intervention followed path-burden escalation
- intervention followed cap-pressure persistence
- intervention followed a live oversight concern with uncertain persistence

### Sleeve-specific restoration

Allowed when:

- intervention was mainly related to stacked-state discomfort
- governance wants diversification restored before full gain posture

### Time-based minimum hold

Required conceptually:

- no same-day de-risk and full restoration churn
- each posture change should survive at least one formal review interval unless an operational incident is resolved cleanly

## Why This Stays Governance-Like

This framework stays governance-like because:

- restoration is review-cleared, not bar-triggered
- posture changes are rare and explicit
- decisions depend on governance states, not hidden alpha conditions
- the sleeves themselves are untouched

## What Re-Engagement Must Not Become

It must not become:

- a replacement for frozen `Risk-Off`
- a hidden regime model
- a sleeve timing overlay disguised as caution policy
- a fast in/out process reacting to every portfolio wobble

## Recommended Default

Recommended operational default:

- de-risk rarely
- when de-risked, restore in staged fashion
- restore `Sleeve #2` before `Sleeve #1` unless the intervention was purely operational
- require explicit governance clearance before full restoration

This is the cleanest framework that remains simple enough to use and hard enough to misuse.
